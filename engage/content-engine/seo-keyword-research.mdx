Keyword Research answers the basic question behind every content decision: **is anyone actually searching for this?** Enter a keyword and get back its search volume along with related terms you may not have thought of.

## Running a Search

1. Select your company from the sidebar
2. Go to **Content Engine** → **Keyword Research**
3. Enter a **Keyword** — for example, "project management software"
4. Choose how to **Sort by**: Volume, CPC, Competition, or Keyword
5. Click **Search**

You get the keyword you typed — marked with a **Seed** chip — plus related keywords, each with its own metrics.

## Reading the Results

| Column | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Volume** | How many searches per month |
| **CPC** | What advertisers pay per click — a proxy for commercial intent |
| **Competition** | How hard the term is to compete for |
| **Trend** | Whether interest is rising or falling |
| **Saved** | Whether you've starred it |

High CPC with moderate volume often beats high volume with no CPC: it means the traffic is worth money to someone.

## Saving Keywords

Tick the keywords worth keeping and click **Save Keywords**. Saved keywords go to [Keywords](/engage/content-engine/seo-keywords), where they're stored with their metrics for later.

## Credits

A lookup costs **25 credits**, and results are cached — repeating a search you've already run is free, and the results line tells you which happened (`25 credits used` or `served from cache, no credits used`).

**Refresh Data** deliberately re-purchases metrics at full cost. Use it when you think the numbers have gone stale, not as a matter of routine.

## Tips

1. **Search the phrase your customer would type**, not your product's internal name.
2. **Mine the related keywords.** The seed term is rarely the best one — the list around it is the point.
3. **Read volume and competition together.** A low-volume term you can actually rank for beats a high-volume term you can't.
4. **Let the cache work.** Re-running a search costs nothing; refreshing costs 25 credits.
